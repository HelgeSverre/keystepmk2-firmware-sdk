# KeyStep MK2000: design

A custom firmware for the 32-key KeyStep mk2, built on the SDK in `sdk/`. Four-track sequencer,
MIDI channels changed with a held button and a numbered key, stock panel meanings kept where
they exist. This file is the specification the code in `sdk/examples/mk2000` follows. What the
stock firmware does with each gesture is in [STOCK-UI.md](STOCK-UI.md).

Status: runs in the emulator, with the engine, store, gestures, pages, arpeggiator, external
clock and MIDI thru in place and checked there (`just test`). Not built: controller recording,
Mutate and Spice, scales and chords, the arp settings page, CV settings. Mutate and Chord do
nothing yet. Nothing has been flashed to a device.

## Hardware it has to work with

- 32 keys, numbered 0 to 31 from the low F. Printed above them: keys 0–3 are **bank keys** 1–4,
  keys 4–19 are **numbered keys** 1–16 (the stock "pattern" keys), keys 20–31 are the Rate and
  Seq Edit keys: Time Div slower (20), faster (21), triplet (22), Nudge − (23), Nudge + (24),
  Octave − (25), Octave + (26), Double (27), Quant (28), Reload (29), Clear (30), Undo (31).
- 13 buttons with a colour light each: Seq, Arp, Mutate, Chord, Tempo, Record, Stop, Play,
  Shift, Hold, Oct −, Oct +, and the encoder's push. Back has no light.
- No lights above the keys (those exist on the 37-key model only).
- A 128 × 32 display, one encoder, two touch strips, aftertouch, a pedal input.
- MIDI in and out on DIN, USB MIDI, pitch CV, gate, two mod CVs, sync in and out.

## Rules

1. Printed legends keep their meaning.
2. Anything done while performing is a held button plus a key. The menu is for setup and for
   fine edits of single steps.
3. The keys always play.
4. All feedback is on the display and the button lights.
5. Stop, Play, Seq and Arp never act on press while another button is held. A combination acts
   when its key is tapped, or when the buttons are released with nothing else pressed. A release
   counts as a **tap** only within 350 ms of the press.
6. Nothing is saved by itself. Shift + Seq saves; a star on the display marks unsaved edits.

## Model

- A **pattern** holds four **tracks** and a tempo. A track has a MIDI channel, a length of 1 to
  64 steps, a time division, swing, gate, direction, a mute flag and its steps.
- A **step** holds up to eight notes with velocities, and gate, probability, ratchet (1–4),
  a condition, a tie flag and an **offset**: when its notes start, from half a step early to half
  a step late, in 256ths of the track's step length (its time division; swing moves the grid, the
  offset moves the step from there). A late step starts at most just before the middle of its own
  step, an early one at most half the step before it early, so the steps of a track keep their
  order; the first step after Play or a pattern change starts on the grid. Ratchets and gate are
  counted from where the step starts, and probability and condition are decided there, for the
  pass the step belongs to; a step starting while the one before still has ratchet hits to play
  drops them. A step that would start early just before a waiting pattern takes over does not
  start: its pass is replaced.
- 64 pattern **slots**: 4 banks of 16. Slots are numbers, not fixed places in flash; as many as
  fit in the SDK's flash area can be used (roughly 16 to 30).
- One **arpeggiator**, on the keys.
- **Seq** lit: the tracks play while the clock runs. **Arp** lit: the keys go through the
  arpeggiator. Independent. Play and Stop are the clock.
- The keys are in one of two states. **Track**: they play the selected track's channel. **Home**:
  they play the home channel. While Record is on they always play the selected track.

## Gestures

"Numbered key" means keys 4–19, counted 1–16.

| Gesture | Does |
|---|---|
| Play, Stop | Start from the top, stop |
| Seq (tap) | Tracks on or off |
| Arp (tap) | Arpeggiator on or off |
| Seq + bank key 1–4 | Select track |
| Seq + numbered key 1–4 | Mute or unmute track 1–4 |
| Seq + numbered key 5–8 | Solo track 1–4 while held |
| Seq + encoder click | Track settings page (stock: long press of Seq) |
| Seq + encoder turn | Step page of the selected track |
| Arp (tap) + keys | Up, Down, UpDown, Random, Order or Poly over the held keys, on the channel the keys go to, in step with the tracks |
| Arp + numbered key 1–6 | Arp mode; Arp + bank key: 1 to 4 octaves. Arp settings page: not built |
| Shift + Stop (tap) | Flip the keys between Home and Track |
| Shift + Stop + numbered key | Home channel 1–16, and switch to Home |
| Shift + Stop + Seq + numbered key | Channel of the selected track |
| Shift + Stop + bank key, then numbered key | Channel of that track |
| Shift + Stop + encoder turn | Home channel |
| Shift + bank key | Bank |
| Shift + numbered key | Pattern 1–16 of the bank; takes over when track 1 wraps |
| Shift + Seq | Save Pattern page: numbered key picks another slot, click confirms |
| Shift + Play | Pattern Chaining page: numbered keys add patterns, Hold takes the last off, Shift + Clear empties it, turn for on/off, click or Back to finish |
| Shift + Tempo | Length of the selected track: encoder, or numbered key (bank key first for the page of 16) |
| Shift + key 20, 21, 22 | Time division of the selected track: slower, faster, triplet |
| Shift + key 23, 24 | Nudge the selected track one step |
| Shift + key 25, 26 | Shift the selected track down or up an octave |
| Shift + key 27, 28, 29, 30, 31 | Double, Quant, Reload, Clear, Undo; on the selected track except Reload (whole pattern). Quant sets every offset of the track to 0 (`Track Quantized`); Undo takes it back. Track settings has Quantize with 50 % (half way) and 100 %, as the stock Seq Settings do |
| Shift + Record | Overdub on or off |
| Shift + Hold | Transpose Hold: the keys transpose the selected track, and the tracks set to follow it (off by default) |
| Tempo + encoder, Tempo (tap) | Tempo, 1 BPM a detent; tap tempo from two to four taps |
| Encoder click, nothing held | Global settings page |
| Record, stopped | Step recording: keys add notes, Hold adds a rest, keys + Hold tie. Back or Record ends |
| Record, playing | Live recording into the selected track, each note into the nearest step; with Rec Quantize Off the first note into a step sets its offset to when it was played |
| Record then Play | A bar of count-in on the display and the Play and Record lights, then live recording. Following an external clock it starts as Play does |
| Hold | Latch the arp, or the played notes when the arp is off; a new chord replaces the latched one |

## Pages

The step page, track settings, global settings and the chain page stay open until Back (the chain
page also closes with a click). The menus look like the stock ones: a small title line, the entry
in the large font, turn to move, click to edit, turn to change, click or Back to finish. The
encoder's light is on while a page is open.

- While the buttons that opened a page are still down, the page has every control: Shift + Play
  with Shift still held sends the numbered keys to the chain, and Seq + turn with Seq still held
  moves along the steps. Once they are let go, the held layers (Shift + Stop, Seq, Shift, Shift +
  Tempo) work over the page as everywhere else and the page comes back when they are released.
- The keys play as usual except where a page uses them: the numbered and bank keys in the list of
  the step page, every key inside a step, the numbered keys in the chain page. Play and Stop always
  work.
- **Step page.** The list: turn to move along the steps of the selected track, a numbered key for
  a step in the page of 16, a bank key for the page; the notes in the large font and the 16 cells
  below. Click enters the step: Velocity (of every note of the step), Gate (5 % steps; 0 is
  "Track", the track's gate), Probability (5 % steps), Ratchet 1–4, Condition (Always, 1 of 2,
  2 of 2, 1 of 3, 1 of 4, First, Not first), Tie and Offset (−50 to +50 % of a step, 5 % steps;
  shown as `-25 %`, `0 %`, `+10 %`). Keys played inside a step replace its notes,
  a chord at a time, and sound on the track's channel. The first edit takes an Undo snapshot.
- **Track settings** of the selected track: Channel, Length, Time Div, Swing (50–75 %), Gate
  (10–90 %, 5 % steps), Direction, Follows transpose, Shift (−48 to 48 semitones), and Quantize (50 % or 100 %; the
  click that ends the edit does it).
- **Global settings**: Brightness of the button lights (1 to 10, 10 being the stock firmware's
  brightest), Pattern change (At the wrap, At once), Tempo on change (When stopped, Always,
  Never), Overdub, Rec Quantize (On, Off; the stock name; On by default), Keys (Track, Home),
  Home channel, Clock Receive (Auto, Internal, USB, MIDI Din, Sync In; the stock firmware's
  name), MIDI Thru (Off, On), USB to MIDI (Off, On), and About (name, version, free flash
  sectors). Written a moment after a change, while stopped; Clock
  Receive and the thru settings act at once. See Sync and routing.
- **Chain page.** The chain of the bank in use, up to 32 patterns, shown in the small font. It is
  saved when the page closes. When it is on and not empty, Play starts on its first pattern and
  each pattern hands over to the next when track 1 wraps, round and round; the same pattern twice
  in a row plays twice. Stop drops the pattern waiting next. Picking a pattern by hand (Shift +
  numbered key or bank key) switches the chain off.

Recording replaces the track unless Overdub is on. The track is cleared only when the first
note is played. Step recording sets the length to the steps entered; a track first recorded live
takes the length of track 1, or 16. A live note goes into the nearest step and sounds as played,
not again from the track in that pass. With Rec Quantize On the first note into an empty step puts
it on the grid (offset 0); with it Off it keeps the time it was played, to the tick; later notes
into the same step keep the step's offset. Step recording always writes offset 0.

## Display

Three text rows and a bar: small font at row 0, large font at row 7, small font at row 22, a
two-row bar at rows 30–31. `sdk/examples/screens` draws them all; `docs/mk2000-screens.png`.

- Home: `Tempo: 120.0` and the time division (`Ext Tempo: 120.0` while following a clock, as
  measured, and `Ext Tempo: --` until one is heard); `Bank: 1 | Preset: 1` with `*` when unsaved; the
  track line (selected track in a box, muted struck through, empty as `--`) with `KBD T2` or
  `KBD 9` at the right; the position bar while playing.
- While a layer is held, its screen replaces Home: MIDI Channel, Tracks, Length, pattern slots.
- An open page replaces Home until it is closed (see Pages).

## Sync and routing

**Clock Receive** says what the 96-per-quarter tick follows.

- **Internal**: its own clock at the pattern's tempo.
- **USB**, **MIDI Din**: MIDI clock on that input, 4 ticks per clock. **Sync In**: pulses on the
  sync jack, one per sixteenth (the rate MK2000 sends), 24 ticks per pulse.
- **Auto** (the default): its own clock until a clock or Start arrives on USB or DIN, or a pulse
  on the sync jack while MK2000's own clock is stopped (an unplugged jack reads the sync output,
  so pulses while it plays could be its own). It follows that input until the input has been
  quiet for 2 s, then goes back to its own clock, playing on at the pattern's tempo if it was.
  Clocks on the other inputs are ignored meanwhile.

Following: the first tick of a clock plays on the clock, the others are spread evenly over the
time between the last clocks (averaged over a few, a jump of more than an eighth taken at once).
No more ticks than a clock is worth run ahead of it; any still owed when the next clock comes
play at once. So no tick is lost or doubled whatever the tempo does, and the tracks and the arp
stay on the incoming clock. MK2000 sends MIDI clock and its sync output from these ticks as
always, so it passes the tempo on.

Transport while following:

- MIDI Start or Continue from the followed input starts from the top on the next clock (there is
  no song position, so Continue does the same); Stop stops. They are ignored from other inputs.
- The sync jack's run line is not used: a cable with the clock only is common, and an unplugged
  jack reads MK2000's own outputs. Play arms and the next pulse is the first step.
- Play on the panel does the same with any external clock: it starts on the next clock or pulse.
  Stop stops at once.

**MIDI Thru** sends what comes in on DIN to the DIN output and to USB; **USB to MIDI** sends what
comes in on USB to the DIN output. Messages are passed on whole, in between the sequencer's own
(running status is written out in full, real-time bytes inside a message go on at once), SysEx
up to 128 bytes included; longer SysEx is dropped. A message that finds no room in the output
buffers waits up to 100 ms, then is dropped rather than sent in part. Not passed on: clock,
Start, Continue and Stop (MK2000 sends its own, so they would arrive twice), and Arturia SysEx
from USB, which is for this unit. Incoming notes do not reach the arp or recording.

## Lights

| Light | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Seq | Colour of the selected track (orange, teal, pink, green); bright when tracks play, dim when off |
| Arp | On when the arp is on |
| Play | Blinks on the beat while running |
| Stop | Dim while stopped |
| Record | Red while recording; blinking on the beat during the count-in; dim when Overdub is on |
| Hold | On when latched; blinking in Transpose Hold |
| Shift, Tempo | On while held |
| Oct − / + | Brighter per octave; no flashing |
| Encoder | On while a page or the Save Pattern page is open |

## Storage

Only the flash area `0x41000–0x5FFFF` (31 sectors of 4 KiB) is written. Patterns are stored
compactly, one or two sectors each; a save writes the new copy before the old one is erased, so
a power cut leaves one valid version. A pattern with no offsets is saved in format 1, the one
from before the offsets, which older builds read too; one with offsets in format 2, which they
report as damaged. Both are read.

## Build stages

1. Stock reference: done ([STOCK-UI.md](STOCK-UI.md)).
2. Engine: tick, tracks, lengths, divisions, swing, gate, direction, ratchet, probability,
   conditions, pattern switch, live record.
3. Store: patterns, chains, settings.
4. Interface: gestures, layers, screens, lights, recording, channels.
5. Arpeggiator, step page, settings menus, chains, controller recording. The pages and chains
   are in `pages.c` and `main.c`.
6. Sync and routing: external clock and MIDI thru done (`clock.c`, `main.c`); CV settings not.
7. Device.
