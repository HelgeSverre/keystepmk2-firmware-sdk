# KeyStep MK2000: design

A custom firmware for the 32-key KeyStep mk2, built on the SDK in `sdk/`: a four-track sequencer,
MIDI channels changed with a held button and a numbered key, the stock panel meanings kept where
they exist. This file is the specification the code in `sdk/examples/mk2000` follows. The guide
for the player is [user-guide.html](user-guide.html), with [USER-GUIDE.md](USER-GUIDE.md) as plain
text; a gesture or setting that changes here changes in both. What the stock firmware does with each
gesture is in [STOCK-UI.md](STOCK-UI.md).

Status, emulator: the engine, store, gestures, pages, arpeggiator, scales, chords, the strips,
aftertouch and pedal with their recording, external clock and MIDI thru are in place and checked
there (`just test`), as are the CV outputs with the stock settings and Spice, Dice, the strips'
Gate and Spice functions, the user scale, vibrato, the unit's CV calibration and the chord
Voicing `On`. Not built from the stock firmware: the chord Spread setting, which has no entry in
the 32-key unit's own menu.

Status, on a unit (one 32-key unit, 2026-11-25):

- Played over USB MIDI into a synth: the keys and recording and playing tracks worked. Its store
  has written to the external flash there; loading a saved pattern back was not checked. One
  fault found there is fixed: a track's note-off cut a note still held on the keys.
- A recorded track that did not play back turned out to be Seq tapped off, which only the dim
  Seq light showed. Home now reads `Seq off` in place of the track line.
- The build flashed last adds the step page's key entry, Spice, Dice and the strips' Gate, the
  user scale, vibrato, CV calibration, Voicing `On`, the corrected aftertouch and pedal inputs
  and the `Seq off` line. None of that has been gone through on the unit.
- Not flashed yet, emulator only: Mutate and Spice by the stock rule, the helix while Mutate is
  held, Arp To Seq, the two step-page fixes and the SDK's own display fonts.
- Never tried on a unit: MIDI on the DIN sockets, external clock, thru, the CV, gate and sync
  outputs.

## Hardware it has to work with

- 32 keys, numbered 0 to 31 from the low F. Printed above them: keys 0–3 are **bank keys** 1–4,
  keys 4–19 are **numbered keys** 1–16 (the stock "pattern" keys), keys 20–31 are the Rate and
  Seq Edit keys: Time Div slower (20), faster (21), triplet (22), Nudge − (23), Nudge + (24),
  Octave − (25), Octave + (26), Double (27), Quant (28), Reload (29), Clear (30), Undo (31).
- 14 buttons. Thirteen have a colour light: Seq, Arp, Mutate, Chord, Tempo, Record, Stop, Play,
  Shift, Hold, Oct −, Oct + and the encoder's push. Back has none.
- No lights above the keys.
- A 128 × 32 display, one encoder, two touch strips, aftertouch, a pedal input.
- MIDI in and out on DIN, USB MIDI, pitch CV, gate, two mod CVs, sync in and out.

## Rules

1. Printed legends keep their meaning.
2. Anything done while performing is a held button plus a key. The menu is for setup and for
   fine edits of single steps.
3. The keys always play.
4. All feedback is on the display and the button lights.
5. Stop, Play, Seq and Arp never act on press while another button is held. A combination acts
   when its key is tapped, or when the buttons are released with nothing else pressed. A release
   counts as a **tap** only within 350 ms of the press.
6. A pattern is saved only by Shift + Seq; a star on the display marks unsaved edits. Settings
   and chains are saved by themselves (see Storage).

## Model

- A **pattern** holds four **tracks**, a tempo (30.0 to 240.0 BPM) and a scale choice. A track
  has a MIDI channel, a length of 1 to 64 steps, a time division, swing, gate, direction, a mute
  flag, a shift in semitones, a Follows transpose flag and its 64 steps. A new pattern: 120.0 BPM,
  tracks on channels 1–4, 16 steps of 1/16, gate 50 %, swing 50 %, direction Forward.
- A **step** holds up to eight notes with velocities, and gate, probability, ratchet (1–4),
  a condition, a tie flag and an **offset**: when its notes start, from half a step early to half
  a step late, in 256ths of the track's step length (its time division; swing moves the grid, the
  offset moves the step from there). A late step starts at most just before the middle of its own
  step, an early one at most half the step before it early, so the steps of a track keep their
  order; the first step after Play or a pattern change starts on the grid. Ratchets and gate are
  counted from where the step starts, and probability and condition are decided there, for the
  pass the step belongs to; a step starting while the one before still has ratchet hits to play
  drops them. A step that would start early just before a waiting pattern takes over does not
  start: its pass is replaced.
- 64 pattern **slots**: 4 banks of 16. Slots are numbers, not fixed places in flash; as many as
  fit in the SDK's flash area can be used (roughly 16 to 30). Choosing an empty slot gives a new
  pattern with the tempo and track channels of the one before.
- One **arpeggiator**, on the keys, with up to 16 notes.
- A **scale** holds the keys to its notes: a key outside it plays the nearest note of the scale,
  the lower one when two are as near (the stock rule). There is a global scale with its root, in
  the settings (Off and C to begin with), and each pattern has its own choice: Global (the
  default), a scale and root of its own, User, or Off. The fifteen scales are the stock ones. The
  scale acts on the keys; notes already in a track are not moved when it changes.
- **Chord** lit: a key plays the chord built on it, from one of the stock presets or the user
  chord. Every note of the chord is fitted to the scale. With the arp on, the arp runs over the
  chord's notes; in a recording the whole chord is recorded.
- **Seq** lit: the tracks play while the clock runs. **Arp** lit: the keys go through the
  arpeggiator, except while recording, when they play and record directly. Independent. Play and
  Stop are the clock.
- The keys are in one of two states. **Track** (the default): they play the selected track's
  channel. **Home**: they play the home channel (1 to begin with). While Record is on they always
  play the selected track.
- Everything MK2000 plays and sends goes out on both DIN and USB.

"Numbered key" means keys 4–19, counted 1–16.

| Shift + Mod strip | **Spice** of the selected track, 0 to 100 % (stock); nothing is sent |

## Pages

The step page, track settings, global settings, CV settings, the arp, chord and scale pages and
the chain page stay open until Back; the chain page also closes with a click. Opening another
page closes the one open. The menus look like the stock ones: one entry at a time, turn to move,
click to edit, turn to change, click or Back to finish. Moving, the page's name is at the top
with `3 of 14` at the right, the entry in the large font and its value below; editing, the
entry's name is at the top and its value in the large font, boxed, with `click: done`. Values
stop at their ends; none wraps. The encoder's light is on while a page is open.

- While the buttons that opened a page are still down, the page has every control: Shift + Play
  with Shift still held sends the numbered keys to the chain, and Seq + turn with Seq still held
  moves along the steps. Once they are let go, the held layers (Shift + Stop, Seq, Shift, Shift +
  Tempo, Arp, Chord) work over the page as everywhere else, with their own screens, and the page
  comes back when they are released.
- The keys play as usual except where a page uses them: every key on the step page (it enters
  its note), the numbered keys on the chain page, every key while the user scale is being edited.
  Play and Stop always work.
- **Step page.** The list shows one step: the track and step at the top, its notes (or `Rest`) in
  the large font and the page of 16 as cells below, the step under a line. The top right reads
  `click: edit` on a rest, `Hold: rest` on a step with notes, `past end` past the track's length.
  - **Turn** moves along all 64 steps, on past the track's length. Steps past the length are
    edited like the others and are silent until the length reaches them; entering a note there
    never changes the length (Shift + Tempo does). They are drawn as dots, a small block when they
    hold a note, with a bar after the last step that plays.
  - **A key** puts its note on the step and sounds on the track's channel; keys held together
    make a chord. The page stays on the step. The keys enter single notes, fitted to the scale;
    chord mode, the arp and Transpose Hold do not apply.
  - **Seq held**: a numbered key goes to that step of the page of 16, the encoder moves 16 steps a
    detent, a bank key selects the track as it does with Seq everywhere. Mute and solo are not on
    this page.
  - **Hold** (or Shift + Clear) makes the step a rest, in the list and inside a step: its notes,
    tie and offset go, its other fields stay.
  - **Click** enters the step; Back leaves it. Its fields: Velocity 1–127 (of every note of the
    step), Gate (0 to 100 % in fives; 0 shows `Track`: the track's gate), Probability (0 to 100 %
    in fives), Ratchet 1–4, Condition (Always, 1 of 2, 2 of 2, 1 of 3, 1 of 4, First, Not first),
    Tie (Off, On) and Offset (−50 to +50 % of a step in fives, shown as `-25 %`, `0 %`, `+10 %`).
    The line below shows the other fields in short. Keys played inside a step replace its notes, a
    chord at a time, and sound on the track's channel.
  - The first edit of a track on the page takes the Undo copy, so Shift + Undo brings back the
    track as it was when the page opened.
- **Track settings** (`T1 Settings`) of the selected track: Channel (1–16), Length (1–64 steps),
  Time Div (1/2D to 1/32T), Swing (50–75 %), Gate
  (10–90 %, 5 % steps), Spice (0–100 %), Direction (Forward, Reverse, Pendulum, Random; Pendulum
  does not repeat its end steps), Follows transpose (Off, On), Shift (−48 to +48 semitones),
  Quantize (50 % or 100 %) and Clear Autom (Bend, Mod, Aftertouch, Pedal, All; All to begin
  with). Quantize and Clear Autom are actions: the click that ends the edit does them.
- **Global settings**, in menu order, defaults in brackets:

  | Entry | Values (default) |
  |---|---|
  | Brightness | 1 to 10 (10): the button lights; 10 is the stock firmware's brightest |
  | Pattern change | At the wrap, At once (At the wrap) |
  | Tempo on change | When stopped, Always, Never (When stopped): when a pattern picked brings its own tempo |
  | Overdub | Off, On (Off) |
  | Rec Quantize | Off, On (On); the stock name |
  | Rec Autom | Off, On (On) |
  | Keys | Home, Track (Track) |
  | Home channel | 1 to 16 (1) |
  | Aft Sensitivity | Off, Low, Medium, High (Medium) |
  | Clock Receive | Auto, Internal, USB, MIDI Din, Sync In (Auto); the stock name |
  | MIDI Thru | Off, On (Off) |
  | USB to MIDI | Off, On (Off) |
  | CV Settings | Click opens its page; Back comes back here (see CV outputs) |
  | About | Click shows the name, version, free sectors (`Free 12/31`) and the CV calibration |

  Clock Receive and the thru settings act at once. See Sync and routing.
- **Arp settings**: Arp Length (1 to 64 steps; 16) and Arp Retrigger (Off, On; On), the two
  entries of the stock page, then Mode (Up), Octaves (1 to 4; 1), Time Division (1/16), Gate
  (10–90 % in fives; 50 %) and Swing (50–75 %; 50 %). With Arp Retrigger on, the pattern starts
  from its beginning again every Arp Length steps; off, it runs on. On stock, a length of 16 that
  has never been edited does not restart the pattern (STOCK-UI.md).
- **Chord page**: Preset (User, Oct, 5th, Maj, min, sus2, sus4, Maj7, min7, Maj9, min9, Maj11,
  min11; User), Strum (ms) (0 to 80 in twos: the time from one note of the chord to the next;
  0), Strum Type (Up, Alt Up, Down, Alt Down, Random; Up) and Voicing (Off, On, Bass -1, Bass -2,
  Bass -3: the root that many octaves down as well; Off). **Voicing On** is the stock rule
  [0x0805CF14]: each note of a chord is moved an octave, or left, towards the range the chord
  before it sounded in, so a run of chords stays in one register; the notes keep the order root,
  then intervals, which is the order the strum walks. Changing Voicing or the preset starts it
  afresh. A key let go while its chord is still being strummed plays no more of it. Two keys
  holding the same pitch: it sounds until the last of them is let go. The user chord holds up to
  eight notes within two octaves of its lowest and starts empty: the key alone.
- **Scale page**: Global Scale (Off and the fifteen scales; Off) and Global Root (C to B; C;
  stock has the root in MIDI Control Center only), Pattern Scale (Off, Global, the fifteen
  scales, User; Global) and Pattern Root (C). The pattern's two are saved with the pattern, the
  global two with the settings. **User**, as on stock: with Pattern Scale open for editing and
  set to User, each key puts its note into the pattern's own scale or takes it out, in any
  octave; the display shows `User Scale`, the twelve notes from the root as cells and the root.
  The root is always in. Until it is edited the user scale holds all twelve notes. Its notes are
  counted from the Pattern Root, so changing the root moves it.
- **Chain page** (`Pattern Chaining`). Each bank has its own chain of up to 32 of its patterns;
  the page shows the chain of the bank in use, `Press pattern keys` while it is empty, how many
  of 32 and `Chain On` or `Chain Off`. It is saved when the page closes. When it is on and not
  empty, Play starts on its first pattern and each pattern hands over to the next when track 1
  wraps, round and round, whatever Pattern change says; the same pattern twice in a row plays
  twice. Switched on while playing, it starts at the next Play; Record then Play does not start
  it. Stop drops the pattern waiting next. Picking a pattern by hand (Shift + numbered key or
  bank key) switches the chain off.

Recording replaces the track unless Overdub is on. The track is cleared only when the first
note is played, and the first note takes the Undo copy. While recording, the arp and Transpose
Hold are bypassed: the keys play and record directly, on the selected track's channel, and the
Home screen's key label reads `REC T1`.

- **Step recording** (Record while stopped). The display shows `T1 Rec` (`T1 Overdub` with
  Overdub on), the step, its notes and the page of 16 as cells. A key or chord fills a step and
  the step advances when the last key is let go; Hold makes a rest; Hold while keys are down ties
  the step into the next. Replacing, it starts at step 1 and the length becomes the steps
  entered; at step 64 it ends. With Overdub on over a track that has notes, it writes over the
  steps from step 1, wraps at the track's length and keeps it, and the encoder moves the step.
  Step recording always writes offset 0.
- **Live recording** (Record while playing, or Record then Play). An empty track recorded live
  gets track 1's length when track 1 has notes, else 16; a track that had notes keeps its length.
  A live note goes into the nearest step and sounds as played, not again from the track in that
  pass. With Rec Quantize On the
  first note into an empty step puts it on the grid (offset 0); with it Off it keeps the time it
  was played, to the tick; later notes into the same step keep the step's offset. Notes played
  during the count-in are not recorded.

## Notes shared by several sources

Every note of the tracks, the keys and the arp goes out through one place that counts, per
channel and note, how many of them are holding it. The note off is sent when the last holder lets
go: a key held on a note that a track also plays is not cut by the track's note off, and two
tracks on one channel do not end each other's notes. A note struck while it is already held is
ended and started again at once, so each attack is heard.

## Spice and Dice

The stock firmware's rule, read from its code ([STOCK-UI.md](STOCK-UI.md), Gate, Spice and Dice;
`pattern.c`). The dice are a seed, and from it every step of the track gets two fixed numbers.
The track's **Spice** says how far they move the step:

| Changed | By |
|---|---|
| Velocity | up to 32 either way at 100 %, turned back at 1 and 127 |
| Gate | anywhere from 1 % to 98 % of the step at 100 % |
| The step is left out | more often the higher Spice is: about one step in five at 100 %, none at 20 % with the gate at 50 % |
| The step plays an octave up or down | the same way: about one in seven at 100 % |

Timing is not touched and the pattern is not changed: Spice works on what is played. A step
varies the same way on every pass, so a spiced track is a fixed variation, not noise. **Dice**
deals every step new numbers. Spice and the dice belong to the track and are saved with the
pattern.

Differences from stock: it keeps one Gate, one Spice and one set of dice per pattern, and here
each track has its own, with the strips acting on the selected track. Its dice are a 16-bit
number and here a byte spread over 16 bits. A Mutate press deals new dice there and does not
here.

## Mutate

The stock firmware's rule, read from its code ([STOCK-UI.md](STOCK-UI.md), Mutate; `mutate.c`).

- **Hold.** While Mutate is held the display shows a double helix turning and the light goes
  round the colours, as stock (`helix.c`). The variation is made when the button is let go.
- **Amount.** A tap under a quarter of a second gives 30 of 100. Held longer, the amount runs
  from 0 to 100 over a second and back to 0 over two thirds of one, and round again; the line
  along the bottom of the picture shows it.
- **From the original.** Every variation is made from the track as it was before the first, never
  from the last variation. Shift + Mutate brings the original back (`Mutate Reset`). Let go at
  amount 0, the variation playing becomes the track (`Variation Kept`).
- **Scale.** The track's own notes say what scale it is in; the Scale setting is not used.
  Pitches move by degrees of that scale, so a variation stays in key.
- **Operations.** Amount / 20 + 1 a round, and four rounds for a track of up to 21 steps, three up
  to 31, two up to 63, one at 64. Each is one of: transpose a stretch by up to three degrees,
  mirror a stretch about a degree, swap two blocks of steps, reverse a stretch, add a harmony
  note (only where the track already has chords), write a run along the scale, move a note into
  a rest. The stretch is longer the higher the amount.
- **What stays.** As many steps play as before. Steps travel whole, with their velocity, gate,
  ratchet and conditions; recorded controllers stay where they are in time. A note that ends
  more than an octave outside the original's range comes back within one.
- A variation always differs from the original.

Saving keeps the variation as the pattern; loading, reloading or changing pattern forgets there
was an original. The Mutate light is on while the selected track is a variation.

Differences from stock: it keeps three hidden copies of the pattern that only block swaps reach,
gives harmony notes a longer gate, and brings a far note back one octave only; none of that is
done here. Host tests (`tests/mutate_test.c`) hold every variation to the rules above; they do
not compare with stock's output, which cannot be reproduced (it seeds from a clock).

## Strips, aftertouch, pedal and CV

They play on the channel the keys go to.

- **Bend strip**: pitch bend, 14 bits, where the finger is; the middle of the strip is the centre.
  Let go, it comes back to the centre in steps over a tenth of a second, as the stock firmware's
  does.
- **Mod strip**: CC 1, and it stays where it was left.
- **Pedal**: sustain, CC 64. Its level at start-up counts as up, so either polarity works when the
  pedal is not held down while switching on.
- **Aftertouch**: channel pressure while a key is down, counted from the level with no key down.
  Aft Sensitivity: Off (nothing is sent), Low, Medium, High.
- **CV**: every note sent on track 1's channel (from any track, the keys or the arp) also plays
  on the pitch and gate outputs. The Bend strip moves the pitch output, and the Mod strip and
  aftertouch feed the mod outputs set to ModWheel or Aftertouch (Mod 2 is ModWheel to begin with),
  whatever channel they play on. Recorded controller values played back by a track go out as
  MIDI only. See CV outputs.

While recording live, with **Rec Autom** on (the stock name; on by default), each of the four is
written into the step the selected track is on, and plays back from that step on every pass: a
value holds until the next step that has one. Bend is recorded with its top 7 bits. Recording a
controller does not clear the track. Track settings has **Clear Autom** (Bend, Mod, Aftertouch,
Pedal or All; the click that ends the edit does it); clearing the track clears them too.

The levels behind aftertouch and the pedal, from the self-test on a unit
([SELFTEST.md](SELFTEST.md)): aftertouch reads 0 to 5 with no key pressed and about 3300 pressed
hard; the pedal socket reads about 4090 with nothing plugged in or a switch pedal up, and about 40
with it down. An expression pedal and a pedal of the other polarity were not tried, so both inputs
are read against their level at rest. MK2000 sending pressure and sustain from them has not been
confirmed on a unit.

## CV outputs

Global settings has **CV Settings**, a page of its own (click to open, Back to come back). Its
entries are the stock firmware's, with its names, values and defaults, and each puts the numbers
on the outputs that the stock firmware did in the emulator (STOCK-UI.md). The outputs have not
been tried on a unit: what those numbers are in volts at the sockets is not known.

The newest note sets the pitch; the gate stays up while any note on track 1's channel is held,
and Stop drops it.

**Calibration.** At start-up MK2000 reads the unit's factory calibration from the stock
firmware's file system on the external flash (`calib/pitch`, `calib/mod1`, `calib/mod2`) and
corrects the three outputs with it exactly as the stock firmware does: `low + value × (high −
low) / 65535`. It only reads (`calib.c` knows enough of the littlefs layout to find three small
files). A file that is missing or does not look like a calibration leaves its output
uncorrected. About shows `CV: calibrated`, `CV: partly` or `CV: no calib`.

| Setting | Values (default) | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch Format | VoltOct, HertzVolt (VoltOct) | VoltOct: ten octaves over the output's range. HertzVolt: a tenth of the range at the reference note, doubling every octave |
| Pitch Ref | C-2 to C3 (C1) | The note at the bottom (VoltOct) or at a tenth (HertzVolt) |
| Pitch Bend Range | 1 to 24 ST (2) | How far the Bend strip moves the pitch output. MIDI pitch bend is not changed by it |
| Gate Format | S-trig, V-trig 5V, V-trig 12V (V-trig 5V) | |
| Gate Retrig | Mono, Legato, Legato Slide (Legato) | A note played over a held one: Mono drops the gate for 2 ms, Legato leaves it up, Legato Slide also glides the pitch there, a twelfth of the way every ms |
| Mod 1 Source, Mod 2 Source | Velocity, Aftertouch, ModWheel, Random, Pulse (Velocity, ModWheel) | Random: a new level at every note. Pulse: full for 10 ms at every note |
| Mod 1 Max Volt, Mod 2 Max Volt | 1 to 10 V (5) | The top of the output: 309 counts of 4095 per volt |
| Wheel Vibrato | 0 to 12.0 st in 0.5 steps (0) | A sine on the pitch output, that deep either way with the Mod strip at the top and in proportion below it. Nothing is sent in MIDI |
| Vibrato Rate | 0.3 to 10.0 Hz in 0.1 steps (1.0) | Its speed |

The menu order is Pitch Format, Pitch Ref, Pitch Bend Range, Gate Format, Gate Retrig, Mod 1
Source, Mod 1 Max Volt, Mod 2 Source, Mod 2 Max Volt, Wheel Vibrato, Vibrato Rate.

The outputs follow a change at once.

## Display

Three text rows and a bar: small font at row 0, large font at row 7, small font at row 22, a
two-row bar at rows 30–31. The fonts are the SDK's two (sdk/README.md, Fonts), made from the X11
Helvetica bitmaps and laid out like the stock firmware's, so the capitals sit on the same rows
as on stock. Their descenders reach two rows lower than stock's, which squeezes g, j, p, q and y
above the line: on the bottom row they reach the bar's rows.

- **Home**, top row: `Tempo: 120.0`, or while following a clock `Ext Tempo: 120.0` as measured
  and `Ext Tempo: --` until one is heard; the scale in force when there is one and room (`D Min`,
  `D Usr` for a user scale); the selected track's time division at the right.
- Middle row: `Bank: 1 | Preset: 1`, with `*` when there are unsaved edits, or the pattern
  waiting to take over followed by `...`. A message (`Pattern Saved`, `Track Cleared`, ...)
  takes this row for 1.2 s.
- Bottom row: the track line: the selected track in a box, an empty track as `--`, a muted or
  soloed-out track struck through; `Seq off` in its place while Seq is off, when no track plays
  whatever its mute. At the right where the keys go: `KBD T2`, `KBD 9` (home channel 9), or
  `REC T2` while recording. Below it, the position of the selected track while playing.
- While a layer is held its screen replaces Home: `Tracks` (Seq), `Bank 1` with the bank's slots
  as cells, filled where a pattern is stored (Shift), `MIDI Channel` (Shift + Stop), `T1 Length`
  (Shift + Tempo), `Arp` with mode, octaves and division (Arp), `Chord` or, while keys are being
  learned, `User Chord` and the notes so far (Chord). `Save Pattern` shows the slot and
  `Replaces a pattern` or `Empty slot`.
- Step recording, the count-in (`Count-in: 4` to `1`) and an open page replace Home until they
  end (see Pages).
- At start-up: `Unofficial`, the version and `KeyStep MK2000`, while each button light comes on in
  turn.

## Sync and routing

**Clock Receive** says what the 96-per-quarter tick follows.

- **Internal**: its own clock at the pattern's tempo.
- **USB**, **MIDI Din**: MIDI clock on that input, 4 ticks per clock. **Sync In**: pulses on the
  sync jack, one per sixteenth (the rate MK2000 sends), 24 ticks per pulse.
- **Auto** (the default): its own clock until a clock, Start or Continue arrives on USB or DIN,
  or a pulse on the sync jack while MK2000's own clock is stopped. It follows that input until
  the input has been quiet for 2 s, then goes back to its own clock, playing on at the pattern's
  tempo if it was. Clocks on the other inputs are ignored meanwhile. The jack counts only while
  stopped because in the emulator's model an unplugged jack reads the sync output; on a unit the
  self-test sent eight pulses with the jack empty and counted none ([SELFTEST.md](SELFTEST.md)).

Following: the first tick of a clock plays on the clock, the others are spread evenly over the
time between the last clocks (averaged over a few, a jump of more than an eighth taken at once).
No more ticks than a clock is worth run ahead of it; any still owed when the next clock comes
play at once. So no tick is lost or doubled whatever the tempo does, and the tracks and the arp
stay on the incoming clock. MK2000 sends MIDI clock and its sync output from these ticks as
always, so it passes the tempo on.

Transport while following:

- MIDI Start or Continue from the followed input starts from the top on the next clock (there is
  no song position, so Continue does the same); Stop stops. They are ignored from other inputs.
- The sync jack's run line is not read: cables with the clock only are common, and what an
  unplugged jack reads on that line is not known on a unit. Play arms, and the next pulse is the
  first step.
- Play on the panel does the same with any external clock: it starts on the next clock or pulse.
  Stop stops at once.

**MIDI Thru** sends what comes in on DIN to the DIN output and to USB; **USB to MIDI** sends what
comes in on USB to the DIN output. Messages are passed on whole, in between the sequencer's own
(running status is written out in full, real-time bytes inside a message go on at once), SysEx
up to 128 bytes included; longer SysEx is dropped. A message that finds no room in the output
buffers waits up to 100 ms, then is dropped rather than sent in part. Not passed on: clock,
Start, Continue and Stop (MK2000 sends its own, so they would arrive twice), and Arturia SysEx
from USB, which is for this unit. Incoming notes do not reach the arp, recording or the CV
outputs.

MK2000 sends MIDI clock on DIN and USB whenever it plays, and the sync output one pulse per
sixteenth, high for half of it, with the run line high while playing.

## Storage

Only the flash area `0x41000–0x5FFFF` (31 sectors of 4 KiB) is written. Patterns are stored
compactly, one or two sectors each; a save writes the new copy before the old one is erased, so
a power cut leaves one valid version. A pattern with no offsets is saved in format 1, the one
from before the offsets, which older builds read too; one with offsets in format 2, which they
report as damaged. Both are read. A pattern with an edited user scale has two more bytes after
its last track, which builds from before the user scale also report as damaged. The settings and
the four chains share one more record, of one sector.

What is saved where:

| Record | Holds | Written |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern, one per slot | Tempo, Pattern Scale, Pattern Root, the user scale, and the four tracks with their settings, mutes and steps (recorded controllers included) | Only by Shift + Seq |
| Settings | Everything in global settings and CV settings, the global scale and root, the arp settings, the chord settings, Seq, Arp and Chord on or off, Overdub, the slot last used | 2 s after the last change, once stopped |
| Chains, one per bank | The chain and whether it is on | When the chain page closes, else once stopped |

Not saved: the selected track, the octave, Hold's latch, Transpose Hold and the transposition,
solo. A failed save shows `Memory Full` or `Save Failed`.
